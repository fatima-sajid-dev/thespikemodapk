How to Spike

Spiking is how most points are won. The key is timing, not power alone:

Time Your Jump

Jump as the ball reaches its peak, not before. Jumping early is the most common reason spikes hit the net.

Use Boom Jump

Boom Jump adds explosive height to your attack. Practice its rhythm in Practice Matches first — the refined timing in v8.0.106 rewards clean execution.

Vary Your Placement

Do not hit the same spot every time. Alternate cross-court and line shots so blockers cannot camp on one angle.

How to Block

Blocking turns the opponent's best weapon into your point. Watch the attacker's approach, not just the ball: their run-up tells you where the spike is going a fraction of a second early, and that fraction is the whole skill. Position yourself at the net early, jump with the attacker rather than after them, and keep your hands high and firm. If you are beaten once, do not chase — reset your position for the next rally instead of leaving the net open twice.

How to Serve

Serving is the only shot you fully control, so make it count. A jump serve adds speed and pressure but needs consistent timing — practice it until ten in a row land before using it in tournaments. A float serve is slower but harder to read, and it is the safer choice in tight sets. Aim for the weakest receiver and for the seams between two players: serves that force a bad first touch win points without any rally at all.

How to Receive

Receiving is the most underrated skill in the game. Stand in a balanced, ready position before every serve, watch the server's hand rather than the ball's early flight, and aim your first touch high and toward the setter — a clean receive makes every attack that follows easier. When facing hard spikes, stay low and absorb the pace instead of swinging at the ball; digging a spike back into play is worth more than a spectacular failed attempt.

After drills, play one tournament match to test what you practiced under real pressure. That loop — drill, then test — is how timing improves week after week.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Jumping too early on spikes. Wait for the ball's peak. 2. Hitting the same spot every time. Blockers adapt fast — vary cross-court and line. 3. Ignoring defense. Matches are won on receives and blocks as much as on spikes. 4. Practicing only in full matches. Matches hide your weaknesses; drills expose and fix them. 5. Switching characters constantly. Learn one main attacker deeply before rotating the roster.
